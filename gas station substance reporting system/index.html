If you feel off

Start here. It takes ten seconds.

You will not get in trouble for calling. Poison Control is not law enforcement, and most states have laws protecting people who call for help during an overdose.

Life-threatening — call now 911 Can't wake them · slow, stopped or gasping breathing · blue or gray lips · seizure · chest pain Not life-threatening — free, 24/7 1-800-222-1222 Poison Control. Nurses and pharmacists who deal with this daily. Confidential, no name required.

Any one of these is a reason to call 911, whatever was taken.

What to tell responders

  1. The exact product name on the package — read it off the label.
  2. How much, and when. A rough answer is fine.
  3. Everything else taken today, including alcohol and prescriptions.
  4. Any conditions or medicines you take regularly.
  5. Bring the package or bottle — it tells them more than a name can.

While you wait

  • Stay with the person. Don't leave them to sleep it off alone.
  • If they're unresponsive but breathing, turn them on their side.
  • If an opioid or tianeptine may be involved and you have naloxone, use it — then still call 911.
  • Don't induce vomiting unless Poison Control tells you to.

Withdrawal can be the dangerous part

Stopping phenibut or other GABA-B drugs abruptly after regular use can cause seizures, hallucinations and delirium — this is a medical emergency, like alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal. Tianeptine withdrawal is opioid-like and, while less immediately life-threatening, is severe enough that people relapse to escape it.

Do not white-knuckle it alone. Tell a clinician exactly what you've been taking and how much — there are protocols for both.

About

Who runs this, and why you can check our work.

StreetMedWatch is a small independent project run by harm-reduction workers, a nurse, and two people who build software. We started it after watching friends and family get hurt by products bought legally, at a counter, with no way to find out what had happened to anyone else.

What we don't do

  • We don't publish doses, sourcing, or anything that reads as instructions.
  • We don't rank substances by "what's trending" — only by reported harm.
  • We don't sell, share, or monetize report data, and we don't run ads.
  • We don't claim a report proves a product caused a reaction. Reports are accounts, not evidence.

Privacy, plainly

Reports are anonymous by design. We do not require an account, we do not store IP addresses with reports, and we strip identifying details before publication. That's also the trade-off: once a report is submitted, we have no way to tie it back to you, which means we can't retrieve or delete it on request.

Alert sign-ups store one email and one state, used only for alerts. Analytics are aggregate and cookie-free.

Funding

Two small public-health grants and individual donations. No industry money — not from supplement makers, smoke-shop distributors, treatment centers, or anyone selling a product mentioned on this site. Annual finances are published in full.

Where our clinical claims come from

  • CDC MMWR reports
  • America's Poison Centers exposure data
  • Peer-reviewed clinical case literature
  • State legislature and health-department records
